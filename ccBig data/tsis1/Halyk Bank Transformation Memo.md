MEMORANDUM
TO: Board of Directors, Halyk Bank FROM: External Product Transformation Auditor DATE: 28.01.2026 SUBJECT: Diagnostic of Product Operating Model Maturity

1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Based on the review of the 2024 Annual and ESG Reports, Halyk Bank is classified as a "Digital Immigrant". While the bank demonstrates strong digital channels and financial performance ("Digitalized Business"), its organizational structure—specifically the separation of technology into "Halyk Lab"—indicates an underlying "IT Service Model" rather than a true Product Operating Model. The bank risks becoming a "Feature Factory" where technology serves the business rather than co-creating value with it.

2. EVIDENCE OF "FEATURE FACTORY" BEHAVIOR (RISKS)
The analysis of the Annual Report (AR) reveals structural patterns that Marty Cagan identifies as critical risks:

The "IT Services" Trap (Halyk Lab):

Evidence: "We founded a new subsidiary 'Halyk Lab'... which specializes in the development... and will provide service maintenance for companies using our software." (AR, Strategic Report).
Diagnosis: Creating a separate entity for IT reinforces the "Mercenaries" mindset. Engineers are treated as vendors (providing service) rather than "Missionaries" (partners solving customer problems). In a true Product Model, engineers and business experts sit on the same team, reporting to a common product leader, not separated by legal entity structures.
Output vs. Outcome Orientation:

Evidence: Success is frequently measured by volume: "17.5 million government services provided," "Number of payments," "Digitalization of services."
Diagnosis: These are Outputs (activity), not Outcomes (value). A Product Organization measures success by customer behavior modification (e.g., "Increased financial health of users," "Retention rate of active users"), not just by how many features or services were shipped or digitized.
Absence of Product Discovery Language:

Evidence: The strategic narrative focuses on "implementation," "digital transformation projects," and "delivery." There is a notable absence of terms like "Product Discovery," "Prototyping," or "Customer Validation" prior to build.
Diagnosis: This suggests a Waterfall approach disguised as digital: Business dictates requirements $\to$ Halyk Lab builds $\to$ Business launches. This increases the risk of building features that do not solve real customer needs.
3. STRATEGIC DISCONNECTS
The Innovation Illusion: The Board's strategy emphasizes being a "Leader in Digital Ecosystems," yet the financial allocation model appears to support a "Project Mindset."

Disconnect: You cannot have a seamless, customer-centric ecosystem when the builders (Halyk Lab) are contractually and structurally separated from the strategists (The Bank). This creates hand-offs, delays, and a "contract negotiation" culture instead of a "collaborative problem solving" culture.
Risk: Innovation becomes slow and expensive because "Business" has to write requirements for "IT" to execute, preventing the rapid iteration required in modern fintech.
4. THE PRESCRIPTION
To shift from a Project-based "Digital Immigrant" to a Product-Led "Digital Native," the Board must mandate the following shifts:

Dismantle the "Vendor" Model: Stop treating Halyk Lab as a service provider. Reintegrate engineers directly into Cross-Functional Product Teams (Pods) aligned with specific customer journeys (e.g., "Mortgage Team," "SME Lending Team")—led by Empowered Product Managers from the business side.
Fund Teams, Not Projects: Abolish annual project budgeting for digital initiatives. Move to Continuous Funding for persistent Product Teams, holding them accountable for quarterly business outcomes (e.g., "Reduce churn by 5%") rather than feature delivery dates.
Redefine "Success": Change executive KPIs. Replace "Number of services launched" with "Product Outcomes" (Customer Lifetime Value, Net Promoter Score specific to feature utility, Time-to-Value for new customers).
